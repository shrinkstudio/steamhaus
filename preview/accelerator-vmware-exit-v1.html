The journey

Assess. Transition. Handover.

No one-click promises. A VMware estate is brownfield by definition — the value is in the decisions, and we're honest about where the mess is.

Phase 01

Assess

AI-driven discovery maps every VM, dependency and licence. We classify each workload: rehost, replatform, or retire. You get a costed migration plan with a target architecture, not a spreadsheet of guesses.

Honest about the workloads that aren't worth moving — sometimes the right call is to retire or rebuild, not migrate.
Phase 02

Transition

Workloads move in waves, lowest-risk first. Where it makes sense, we replatform onto native AWS — containers, managed databases — rather than relocating VMs as-is. Each wave is reversible until you sign it off.

Honest about the brownfield reality — legacy dependencies surface mid-migration. We plan for it; we don't pretend it won't happen.
Phase 03

Handover

The estate lands in your AWS accounts, documented and observable. Your team gets the runbooks, the architecture decisions, and the operational model. Or we keep running it — that's the Managed Service.

Honest about capability gaps — we flag where your team will need upskilling, and we don't leave until the handover sticks.

Questions

What CTOs ask before committing.

Straight answers — also the questions search engines and AI assistants surface when someone's researching a VMware exit.

QHow long does a VMware to AWS migration take?
For a typical mid-size estate (100–300 VMs), 3–6 months end to end. The Assess phase takes 2–4 weeks; the bulk of the time is in phased transition, where we move workloads in low-risk waves. Larger or more regulated estates take longer — we'll give you a real timeline after the assessment, not a sales figure.
QIs this just a lift-and-shift to EC2?
No — and that's the point. Lift-and-shift relocates your VMs and keeps every operational problem you had. We assess each workload for whether it should be rehosted, replatformed onto native AWS services, or retired entirely. Typically 30–40% of workloads are worth replatforming, which is where the long-term cost and capability gains come from.
QWhat happens to workloads that can't move easily?
Every estate has them — old dependencies, undocumented integrations, licensing tied to specific hardware. We surface these in the Assess phase rather than hitting them mid-migration. Some get rebuilt, some get retired, and occasionally the right answer is a hybrid approach for a specific workload. We're honest about the brownfield reality.
QHow much does it cost, and is AWS funding available?
The engagement is procured through AWS Marketplace and scoped after the assessment. Most VMware Exit projects qualify for AWS Migration Acceleration Program (MAP) funding, which can offset a significant portion of migration cost. We handle the funding application as part of the engagement — it's not an afterthought.
QWhere does the AI actually help, versus marketing spin?
AWS Transform does the discovery and dependency mapping across thousands of workloads — work that would take a human team weeks, done in days and more thoroughly. Claude drafts migration runbooks and infrastructure-as-code. What the AI doesn't do is decide your target architecture or sequence the migration waves — those are judgment calls our senior engineers make, reading the tooling's output critically.
QWhat do we get at handover?
The estate lands in your own AWS accounts — fully documented, observable, with runbooks and the architecture decisions written down. Your team can run it, or we can through our AWS Managed Service. There's no Steamhaus control plane to stay locked into; it's standard AWS underneath.
